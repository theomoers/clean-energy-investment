HETEROGENEITY ESTIMATOR AND BALANCE UPGRADE
2026-11-07

BOTTOM LINE

Yes, measured subgroup covariate balance can improve substantially without
dropping evaluation areas. But the weighting changes do not achieve the full
prespecified balance/support standard, and the improved adjusted-score tests
still do not establish reliable political-effect heterogeneity.

This is more informative than the previous raw high-minus-low comparison:
we now evaluate the fixed forest ranking with augmented, covariate-adjusted
investment-change scores, use a continuous ranking test, and summarize effect
modification in a state-adjusted projection. The old raw subgroup audit was
not itself a definitive test of a properly adjusted conditional forest.

The forest remains a genuine exploratory ML component. The evidence does not
support declaring causal heterogeneity, zero effects, or homogeneous effects.

WHAT CHANGED

- Retained the exact original eligible panel: 196 areas in 25 states.
- Preserved the original honest, training-only forest ranking; no new search
  over trees, modifiers, seeds or outcome significance.
- Preserved the state-disjoint split: 95 training areas in 15 states; all 101
  evaluation areas in ten different states. All continuous tests use those
  101 evaluation areas, rather than selecting a small high-effect group.
- Fitted separate Republican/Democratic ridge outcome-change models with nine
  baseline covariates and partially pooled training-state effects. All fitting
  and leave-one-training-state-out tuning use only the 95 training observations.
- An unseen evaluation state's intercept is not learned: its deviation is
  set to zero. This is a material modeling limitation, not hidden knowledge.
- Constructed AIPW-type conditional difference-in-changes scores, tilted toward
  the overlap population. Used bounded score algebra to avoid numerical
  inverse-propensity explosions.
- Refitted propensities in 999 evaluation-state bootstrap draws, holding the
  independent training data, outcome models and ranking fixed.
- Evaluated the continuous ranking by the exact weighted QINI integral;
  positive QINI would mean that higher predicted effects correspond to higher
  adjusted effects. QINI is a ranking statistic, not an average policy effect.
- Estimated a secondary state-adjusted projection of adjusted effects onto
  standardized log geographic area and log prior clean capacity.
- Audited pooled subgroup-aware propensity models and capped calibration
  weights, with design selection based on X/party/frozen scores only.

The outcome-free rank-half definition uses the weighted median of independent
evaluation priorities. It does not use evaluation investment outcomes.
It changes the group definition/target rather than changing the forest ranking.

Manufacturing / rank halves        .071              .514           5.11

Each retains all 101 evaluation observations with positive weights. The table
is a design diagnostic, not a selection of significant outcome estimates.
The earlier worst measured covariate SMDs were 1.18, 1.13 and .46, respectively.
Clean power's smallest effective subgroup/party sample was previously 3.67.

However:
- State composition remains imbalanced.
- A single observation can receive about 30% of a party/group's normalized
  weight in the clean-power and manufacturing examples.
- Some state/group cells have only one observed party. Shrinkage borrows
  information; it does not generate actual comparison observations.
- Keeping every row is not the same as retaining its full statistical weight.

CAP/ESS-CONSTRAINED CALIBRATION

Reported all 42 prespecified design candidates: two definitions x three
outcomes x four pooled-propensity penalties/three calibration tolerances.
No outcome-based selection occurred.

Calibration jointly targets nine covariates and state indicators within groups,
keeps a positive weight floor, and caps weights so Kish ESS is at least five
or one-third of the cell count. Unlike the previous repair, it does not require
the same states to contribute both parties to both high/low groups.

The strict .10-SD target is infeasible for all six outcome/group-definition
cases UNDER THESE CAPS AND POSITIVE-WEIGHT CONSTRAINTS. This is not a proof
that no conceivable weighting scheme exists.

Relaxed .20-SD balance is feasible for capacity (minimum cell ESS about nine).
Relaxed .30-SD balance is feasible for clean-power rank halves and both
manufacturing definitions. These are weaker design standards and are reported
as sensitivity diagnostics, not substituted for the prespecified primary rule.

No candidate meets every primary requirement (including state balance, weight
concentration and support). Therefore no subgroup-aware design is promoted
to a new causal group-effect estimate.

Balance thresholds are diagnostics, not causal theorems. Failing raw balance
does not by itself invalidate a correctly specified adjusted-score estimator;
conversely, passing mean balance would not establish absence of confounding.

These coefficients concern association with the political effect, not causal

IDENTIFICATION AND UNCERTAINTY LIMITATIONS

The estimand remains a Republican-minus-Democratic relative response to the
House majority transition. Both parties change alignment status. It is not
an IRA/45X credit effect, nor gaining alignment versus an unaffected group.

Augmented scores do not establish conditional parallel trends or eliminate
unobserved confounding. The overlap target depends on estimated propensities;
do not describe the entire overlap-targeted procedure as automatically doubly
robust or fully orthogonal. In-sample state-adjusted propensity fitting is
not a fully cluster-cross-fitted nuisance scheme. Bootstrap inference is
conditional on the independent training sample/models/ranking.

Only ten states contribute independent evaluation clusters. Pre-period
outcomes also enter design. Nonsignificant placebo/ranking tests are not
equivalence tests or evidence of no economically meaningful differences.

All upgrades are exploratory because previous results and this dataset were
already inspected. Fresh computational reproduction is not external empirical
replication or a new exogenous political shock.

RECOMMENDATION

Retain the upgraded adjusted-score evaluation: it is better aligned with
testing conditional heterogeneity than the earlier raw subgroup differences.
Use the balancing frontier to document which improvements are possible
without hard drops. Do not claim that the remaining state/support problem
has been resolved or that political heterogeneity has been detected.

Before making a stronger causal claim, the most useful next inputs are
geography-consistent pre-treatment resource/grid/industrial/socioeconomic
measures and predetermined exposure to IRA incentives, plus additional
independent political variation where comparable trends are defensible.
Further significance-driven estimator searches would not supply that evidence.
